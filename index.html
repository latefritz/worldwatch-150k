WHAT WE KNOW

There is already an acquisition engine.

Some Meta campaigns are reporting low acquisition costs. I'd start by understanding which campaigns bring in paying households who stick around, then build on those.

And retention matters. Most subscribers are monthly (92.3%). Only 7.7% are annual, and choosing annual requires contacting member services. That's worth exploring.

WHAT WE STILL DON'T KNOW

Free trials aren't paying subscribers.

We have 1,249 average monthly free trials. If half convert, that's about 625 paying households from those trials. At our assumed 40K active base, we'd still need roughly 795 more additions each month from direct subscriptions, reactivations or other sources just to offset 1,420 cancellations.

That's only a 50% conversion illustration. The actual rate is unknown. So are total monthly paid additions, the CPA definition, and whether the reported $1.5M spend covers a year and WORLD Watch alone.

We cannot conclude the subscriber base is shrinking based on trials alone. There could be other paid signup sources. I'd settle these questions in the first week, not turn this presentation into a data audit.

My takeaway: Find the true trial-to-paid rate, identify every source of paying households, and check how quickly we're replacing cancellations. That tells us how much more acquisition—or retention improvement—we really need.

03 / TWO GROWTH ENGINES, AT THE SAME TIME

Push what's working.
Build what comes next.

I'd put equal effort into two growth engines: making paid advertising, partnerships, and WORLD's existing audiences work harder, while building organic discovery through search, YouTube, and AI. The goal isn't simply to spend more on ads. It's to get more paying households from what we're already doing—and create new ways for families to find WORLD Watch without a matching increase in ad spend. We'd measure both by subscribers gained and kept, not just clicks or free trials.

ENGINE 1 · PAID / PARTNERS / OWNED AUDIENCES

Get more paying households from channels we already know.

Don't replace a campaign because its cost per trial looks high—or scale one just because it looks cheap. Follow it through to paid and retained subscribers.

  • Meta: compare the 7-, 30- and 90-day offers using paid conversion, 30/60/90-day retention and acquisition cost.
  • Podcasts and influencers: use unique landing pages, tracked links and cohort results; double down on audiences that actually stay.
  • Existing WORLD audiences: test relevant placements in newsletters, WORLD Magazine and other channels; measure incremental new WORLD Watch households.
  • The welcome experience: get families watching quickly, then give them a reason to keep coming back.
Don't forget the subscriber journey ↗
ENGINE 2 · AI / GOOGLE / YOUTUBE / PARTNERSHIPS

Build discovery that doesn't depend on buying every click.

WORLD Watch already has a YouTube audience and a deep video library. I'd turn that head start into a steady publishing system that reaches families through YouTube, search and AI discovery—then leads them toward a free episode.

  • Onsite: answer real parent and educator questions with useful pages, transcripts or summaries, and free-episode calls to action.
  • Offsite: get mentioned by trusted homeschool, family and education publications through genuinely useful partnerships.
  • YouTube: test two longer videos and 4–6 Shorts each week from approved reporting, with AI-assisted titles and descriptions and a clear path to a free WORLD Watch episode.
  • Measure: organic and AI referrals, free-episode viewers, trials and paying subscribers—not speculative CPA figures.
My organic discovery tests ↗

Different cost structures: The paid engine needs cost-per-paying-subscriber and retention analysis. The organic engine mainly starts with staff time, editing and outreach—real sweat equity, not free traffic. I'd track those hours and actual signups, not make up a CPA or subscriber forecast.

04 / WHAT I WOULD ACTUALLY TEST ORGANICALLY

Put the content
to work in more places.

Use the reporting WORLD Watch already produces. Build a reliable YouTube schedule, turn strong stories into Shorts, and support the best topics with helpful search pages for parents and teachers.

ON THE SITE · SEARCH + AI

Answer a specific need.

Start with a handful of real questions: news for middle schoolers, current events for homeschool families, and how to talk about difficult news with kids.

  • Review which useful pages already exist; improve those before building duplicates.
  • Create a small set of original guides, accurate episode summaries, accessible video and family discussion prompts.
  • Check indexability, internal linking, headings, metadata and appropriate video structured data.
  • Lead to a relevant free episode, then measure the path to a trial and paid.
First test: 5–10 high-quality pages; baseline their search visibility and conversion.
OFF THE SITE · REAL REFERENCES

Get in front of trusted audiences.

I'd build a list of relevant homeschool organizations, Christian parenting publishers, educators and curriculum resources.

  • Offer a topical free episode or a genuinely useful classroom/family discussion guide.
  • Pitch specific useful resources to existing roundup pages and education newsletters.
  • Track placements and referrals, then see whether those families actually subscribe.
  • Avoid paid link exchanges and generic guest posts just to get a backlink.
First test: 20 thoughtful outreach contacts and a small group of real partnerships.
YOUTUBE · A REAL WEEKLY SCHEDULE

Publish twice a week. Multiply each story.

With an established channel and existing footage, I'd run an eight-week test of a consistent schedule, rather than rely on occasional uploads.

  • 2 longer videos each week: 4–10-minute, editor-approved explainers—one evergreen question and one timely topic when a good fit.
  • 4–6 Shorts each week: standalone answers pulled from those videos or the existing library, not six new shoots.
  • AI assists production: research topic demand, draft accurate titles, descriptions, chapters and thumbnail concepts for human review.
  • Give viewers a next step: use related videos and eligible description/channel links to offer a relevant free WORLD Watch episode.
  • Measure the right outcome: watch time, returning viewers, staff hours, free-episode visits, trials and new paying households.
First test: 8 weeks at this pace; keep the formats that turn viewers into lasting subscribers.
WHY I'D PUT REAL WORK INTO THIS

I've seen what happens when AI traffic starts bringing customers back.

At my client PackagingSupplies.com, we're now seeing about $12K a month in LLM (AI) referral sales and another $5K a month from Google AI Overview. That's $17K in monthly sales from first-time purchasers—roughly a 7% increase in new sales each month. But here's where it starts to snowball: those customers come back and buy again. Once we include their repeat purchases, AI-driven customers now account for around 15% of monthly sales, and that share is growing. Before we began our AI/GEO work, attributable AI sales were virtually nonexistent. WORLD Watch is an even more content-heavy business, which makes this worth testing—without promising the same results.

$12K + $5KLLM referrals + Google AI Overview · first-time sales per month~7% new sales · ~15% including repeat purchases

These are client-reported attributed sales, including repeat purchases from AI-acquired customers; the figures do not prove incremental lift or forecast results for WORLD Watch. Organic work has labor and production costs. Google's AI-search guidance still centers on useful, crawlable content; there is no special AI-only schema that guarantees visibility.

05 / KEEP FAMILIES LONGER, AND INCREASE THEIR VALUE

Don't let the relationship
stop at the signup.

WORLD may already have some or all of these email and messaging sequences. Great—I'd start by seeing what exists and how it performs, then improve the gaps. The goal is better trial conversion, more viewing, fewer cancellations, and more value from families who already trust WORLD.

01 · TRIAL WELCOME

Get them to the first episode.

Immediately send the easiest way to watch. Follow up with a great story or family discussion prompt. Help those who signed up but haven't watched yet.

Measure: first-episode starts, trial → paid.
02 · NEW MEMBER / POST-PURCHASE

Help the new subscriber build a habit.

After payment, show them where to watch, what to watch next and how to use WORLD Watch with kids. Later, introduce a genuinely relevant WORLD offer when appropriate.

Measure: viewing, retention, cross-sell revenue.
03 · REPEAT ENGAGEMENT / CROSS-SELL

Give established members another reason to stay.

Send useful episode roundups, relevant subject collections, seasonal homeschool resources and carefully chosen other WORLD products—not endless promotional emails.

Measure: return viewing, renewals, added revenue per household.
04 · AT-RISK / CANCEL SAVE

Catch problems before a family leaves.

Test a re-engagement message when viewing drops, billing reminders when payment fails, and a helpful cancellation feedback path. Explore a simpler annual upgrade if the software allows it.

Measure: recovered payments, saved subscriptions, churn.
05 · WINBACK

Bring former members back.

Split past subscribers by why they left. For the right families, send a timely story, new feature or back-to-school reminder—not the same generic discount to everyone.

Measure: reactivated paying households, second-term retention.
Important distinction: Selling an existing WORLD Watch household another WORLD product can improve overall customer lifetime value, but it does not count as a new WORLD Watch household toward 150K. Reactivated WORLD Watch subscribers can count as paid additions if they weren't already active.
